# Eval Scenario GS-150: Ordering a paged list never repeats or skips a row

PRD-20260916-catalog-batch-f4-review-sorting (§十 of the catalog upgrade plan): the store review list was locked to newest-first, so on a product with hundreds of reviews the lowest ratings — the ones a shopper actually wants before buying — could only be reached by paging through everything. F-4 turns the hardcoded order into a whitelisted parameter and gives the PDP a sorting control, without touching the pagination envelope, the rating distribution or the approved-only rule.

## MUST do
- Whitelist the orderings and fall back to the default for unknown or blank values rather than surfacing internal values through error codes
- End every ordering with a unique tie-break so a paged walk over rows that share a rating and a timestamp neither repeats nor skips them
- Echo the ordering that was actually applied in the response metadata, keeping every pre-existing metadata key intact
- Keep the aggregate (distribution, average, count) computed over the whole approved population so it is identical under every ordering and on every page
- Refetch the first page when the shopper changes the ordering instead of merging a new order into already-appended pages, and keep the visible list when that refetch fails
- Leave the default path behaviourally identical to the pre-change release

## MUST NOT do
- Accept an arbitrary order string (SQL-level ordering from user input) or return a 4xx that reveals the allowed values
- Sort by a column that is not unique without a tie-break, or recompute the aggregate from the current page
- Let a new ordering mix with pages fetched under the old one, or collapse the list when a refetch fails
- Change which records are public (only approved) or alter the pagination contract's existing keys
- Ship the control without localized labels for every supported locale

## Scoring
- pagingStability: 30
- aggregateIntegrity: 25
- compatibility: 20
- frontendBehaviour: 15
- inputWhitelisting: 10
