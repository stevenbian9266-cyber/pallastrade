# Eval Scenario GS-155: Payment cost is a model you can drill into, not a number you hope for

PRD-20260916-payments-d13c-fee-cost-report (slice 3 of the D13 reconciliation batch, business plan §70.3): the platform knew what it charged and, after importing a settlement file, what the provider had actually deducted — but it had no rate-card model, so nobody could answer what a payment should cost, which checkout entry was the expensive one, or whether the contracted rate still held. This slice adds the fee policy registry (percent + fixed + platform + cross-border + currency-conversion components, scoped by provider / entry / card type / region with an effective window and min/max clamping) and a strictly read-only cost report that prices completed payments, ranks cost by entry, drills from any entry down to its individual payments and puts the modelled fee next to the provider-reported one.

## MUST do
- Resolve exactly one applicable policy per payment with a documented, deterministic priority (entry > provider > store > fallback, then later effective_from, then higher id) and reuse that same ordering in the UI and the resolver
- Scope the variable part (percent + platform + cross-border + conversion) with min/max and add fixed fees afterwards, so a small payment cannot be charged a negative or unbounded rate
- Never invent a cost: when no policy matches, or a declared card-type / region / cross-border / conversion condition cannot be proven from local data, leave the payment unpriced and record why
- Keep the whole feature read-only over local facts — the report and the calculator must not create payments, refunds, ledger rows or inventory movements, and must not call the provider
- Make every report total reproducible from the drilled-down rows, and let an entry's ranking row drill into exactly the payments that produced it
- Keep the reported figures scoped to the store and to completed payments over a half-open period, and keep query count flat as the period grows
- Compare modelled cost with the settlement-ledger fee when it exists, and show the variance instead of silently trusting either side
- Ship the fee policy registry as a soft-revoke audited admin page with the same filtered scope and counts, and export a CSV that carries order and amount facts but no credentials or card data

## MUST NOT do
- Write a fee into the payment, refund, ledger or order path, or make the report a source of truth for money movement — the modelled fee is an estimate for analysis only
- Report a cost for a payment whose rate cannot be established, or present a guessed card type / region / currency basis as if it were known
- Show a total that cannot be reproduced from the drill-down rows, or rank entries in a non-deterministic order when costs tie
- Leak another store's payments, include non-completed payments, or treat a half-open period boundary as inclusive
- Put credentials, card numbers or provider secrets into the report page, the CSV export or the audit payload, and do not let the pages bypass the payment-cost permission
- Claim per-payment historical entry attribution that the schema does not store — the entry dimension is a current-mapping reading and must be labelled as such
